>***Shadow Leap.*** While in Dim Light or Darkness, the oblivion leaper can use its movement to teleport to another space in Dim Light or Darkness. Every foot of teleportation costs the oblivion leaper 1 foot of movement.  
>
>***Sunlight Weakness.*** While in sunlight, the oblivion leaper has Disadvantage on D20 Tests.  
>
>### Actions
>***Soul Drain.*** *Melee Attack Roll:*  +5, reach 5 ft. *Hit:* 7 (2d4 + 3) Necrotic damage, and if the target is a living creature, it is subjected to the following effect. *Constitution Saving Throw:* DC 11. *Failure:* The target loses 1 Hit Die. Hit Dice lost in this way return at the end of a Short Rest, and can be spent to regain Hit Points during that rest as normal.  
>If this effect reduces the target's number of available Hit Point Dice to fewer than 0, the target has the Unconscious condition. At the end of each of its turns, the Unconscious target repeats the save, awakening on a success. If an oblivion stalker kills the creature while it has no Hit Dice, the target's soul is lost to the realm of shadow and can't be revived without mighty magic, such as a *Wish* or Divine Intervention.  
>
>### Bonus Actions
>***Shadow Stealth.*** In Dim Light or Darkness, the oblivion leaper can take the Hide action as a Bonus Action.  
>
> ##### Variant: Customizing Oblivion Stalkers
>
>- Since oblivion stalkers can drain almost any living creature's energy, and they can take just about any shape. Some oblivion stalkers retain the natural abilities of their original form. For instance, an oblivion stalker that was once a wolf might retain the Pack Tactics trait. Similarly, a boar could possess a version of Charge, draining its prey while adding Slashing damage and the possibility of knocking their victim Prone.
>- Keep one special ability that makes sense for a creature of the stalker's former form without affecting the Challenge of the stalker. Avoid bestowing an oblivion stalker any ability that is magical, grants the stalker a ranged attack, or expands its Resistances or Immunities.
